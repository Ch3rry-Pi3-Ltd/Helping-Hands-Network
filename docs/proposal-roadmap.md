# Proposal roadmap

Reviewed: 7 July 2026.

This roadmap turns the ideas from the Digital, Data and AI Opportunity Review
into practical next steps. It is deliberately staged so the charity can keep
control of facts, consent, safeguarding and workload.

The website prototype is the first delivery strand. The other ideas below are
not commitments to build systems yet; they are a safe way to prepare options
for Susan and the trustees.

## Roadmap summary

| Area | Current status | Safe work completed or ready | Needs Susan/trustee input |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Website refresh | Prototype live on Vercel. | Modern Next.js prototype, quality checks, analytics, privacy page, launch docs. | Final content, donation routes, image permissions, domain launch approval. |
| Donation journeys | Public routes have been audited for reachability. | Donation-route map template added. | Which campaigns/forms are current, who owns each route, preferred primary donation action. |
| Impact reporting | Website has a prepared Updates/Impact structure. | Impact template added for evidence, approvals and publication status. | What impact data is already available and who can approve it. |
| Content maintenance | Workflow documented. | Simple review process and handover checklist prepared. | Named content owner and charity approver. |
| Analytics | Vercel Web Analytics enabled. | Privacy-friendly aggregate analytics in place. | Decide whether future conversion/referral measurement is needed. |
| Search visibility | Technical SEO baseline is strong. | Sitemap, metadata, Lighthouse audit and launch checklist complete. | Final domain and Google Search Console access after launch approval. |
| Google/charity tools | Not started. | Readiness checklist can be completed without changing live systems. | Eligibility, admin owner and appetite for ongoing management. |
| AI-assisted admin | Not implemented. | Policy and risk boundaries can be drafted first. | Agreement on acceptable uses, data rules and human review. |
| Volunteer/member tracking | Not implemented. | Scope should start with data minimisation and safeguarding requirements. | What data is truly needed, who accesses it and retention rules. |
| Grant workflow | Not implemented. | Lightweight tracking template can be prepared. | Grant priorities, reusable charity information and named owner. |

## Suggested two-week development focus

The useful work while Susan is busy is preparation, not public publication.

1. Keep the website technically stable and ready for review.
2. Prepare simple templates for impact updates, donation ownership and future
   content updates.
3. Avoid adding unapproved statistics, testimonials, personal information or
   current operational claims.
4. Package the prototype and roadmap so Susan can review it quickly when she
   has time.

## Proposed next decisions

When Susan is available, the most useful decisions are:

- Which donation route should be the primary public call to action?
- Which current contact details should be published?
- Which programme descriptions are accurate now?
- Which photos and quotes are approved for continued use?
- Who should approve public website updates?
- Is the charity interested in developing impact reporting, Google for
  Nonprofits, grant tracking or AI-assisted admin as separate follow-up work?

## Guardrails

- Do not change the live WordPress site, domain, DNS, email or fundraising
  systems without explicit approval.
- Do not publish impact statistics until the source and meaning are confirmed.
- Do not process sensitive beneficiary, volunteer or health-related information
  in AI tools without a separate data-protection and safeguarding decision.
- Keep Susan's administrative burden low: prefer lightweight templates before
  introducing new software.
